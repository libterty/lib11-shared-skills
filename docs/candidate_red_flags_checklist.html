🚩 Candidate Red Flags Checklist

Purpose: This checklist helps engineering managers quickly identify potential warning signs during interviews. Use this as a reference guide during or immediately after interviews to spot concerning behaviors, attitudes, or responses that may indicate poor cultural fit or performance risks. Not every flag is disqualifying, but multiple flags warrant careful consideration.
⚠️ Important Note: Red flags should be considered in context and alongside positive indicators. A single flag may not be disqualifying, but patterns of concerning behavior deserve attention. Always document specific examples and avoid bias by focusing on objective observations. Every flag below is defined by what you directly observe in the interview — not by inference from a candidate's résumé, career history, or background.

🔧 Technical Competence Core Skills Assessment
Cannot explain their own code or projects
Unable to articulate design decisions or walk through code they claim to have written
Example: "I don't really remember how I implemented that feature" when asked about a recent project
Fundamental knowledge gaps for their experience level
Missing basic concepts that should be well-understood at their claimed experience level
Example: Senior engineer unable to explain basic OOP concepts or REST principles
Unwilling to admit knowledge limitations
Makes up answers or refuses to acknowledge when they don't know something
Example: Provides confident but incorrect technical explanations rather than saying "I'm not sure"
No understanding of trade-offs
Cannot discuss pros and cons of technical decisions or different approaches
Example: "This is the only way to do it" without considering alternatives or context
Overly focused on specific tools/frameworks
Cannot think beyond their preferred technology stack or adapt to new tools
Example: "I only work with React" when asked about other frontend approaches
No curiosity about learning
Shows no interest in new technologies, industry trends, or professional development
Example: "I don't need to learn anything new, I know enough already"
💬 Communication & Collaboration Team Fit Assessment
Disparages previous teams or colleagues
Speaks negatively about former teammates, managers, or companies without professional context
Example: "My last team was full of idiots" or "Management never understood anything"
Unable to accept feedback or criticism
Defensive when receiving suggestions or becomes argumentative about alternative approaches
Example: Gets visibly upset when interviewer suggests improvements to their code
Poor communication skills
Cannot clearly explain technical concepts or struggles with basic professional communication
Example: Rambling, unclear explanations that don't address the question asked
Takes all credit, avoids responsibility
Claims sole credit for team successes but blames others for failures
Example: "I built that entire system myself" or "That failure wasn't my fault"
Dismissive of non-technical colleagues
Shows disrespect or condescension toward product managers, designers, or other roles
Example: "Product managers don't understand anything technical"
No examples of collaboration
Cannot provide concrete examples of working effectively with others
Example: All stories are about individual work with no mention of teamwork
⚡ Work Ethic & Attitude Motivation Assessment
Shows up unprepared or late
Late to interview, hasn't researched the company, or unprepared for basic questions
Example: "I didn't have time to look at your website" or arrives 15+ minutes late without explanation
Vague or evasive when asked about role transitions
When asked directly why they left a previous role, cannot give a clear, specific answer even after a follow-up. This flags the clarity of their answer in the interview — not the number of past role changes, since transition patterns often reflect circumstances (layoffs, caregiving, relocation, health, visa status) unrelated to job performance.
Example: Repeats "it just didn't work out" for multiple roles with no concrete detail, even when asked to elaborate
No expressed interest in the work itself when asked directly
When directly asked what interests them about the role or team, cannot articulate anything beyond compensation — even after being given room to elaborate. This flags what they say when prompted, not an assumption about their financial priorities.
Example: When asked "what excites you about this role," says only "the pay is good" and has nothing further to add despite a follow-up prompt
Unrealistic expectations
Expects immediate senior role, full remote work, or other demands that don't match their experience
Example: Junior developer expecting staff engineer title and responsibilities
No questions about the role or company
Shows no curiosity about the work, team dynamics, or company culture
Example: "No, I don't have any questions" when given the opportunity
Rigid about processes and methodologies
Insists their way is the only correct approach, unwilling to adapt to team practices
Example: "If you don't do TDD, you're doing it wrong" without considering context
🧠 Problem-Solving & Growth Capability Assessment
Cannot think through problems systematically
Jumps to solutions without understanding the problem or considering edge cases
Example: Starts coding immediately without asking clarifying questions
No examples of learning from failures
Cannot discuss mistakes they've made or how they've grown from challenges
Example: "I don't make mistakes" or cannot think of any professional failures
Gives up too easily on problems
Doesn't persist when facing difficult questions or coding challenges
Example: "I don't know" without attempting to reason through the problem
Fixed mindset about abilities
Believes skills are innate rather than developed, shows no growth orientation
Example: "I'm just not good at algorithms" without showing effort to improve
Cannot handle ambiguity
Needs very specific requirements and cannot work with incomplete or changing information
Example: Gets frustrated when problem requirements aren't perfectly specified
